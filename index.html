A Beginner's Guide to Eating Healthy

You don't need a strict diet or expensive ingredients. A few simple habits go a long way.

By the Everyday Wellness team · 6 min read

Ask ten people what "eating healthy" means and you'll get ten answers. The good news is that most nutrition advice comes down to a few ideas that hold up no matter which trend is popular this year. Here is a simple place to start.

Build a balanced plate

Instead of counting every calorie, picture your plate at each meal. Fill about half with vegetables and fruit, a quarter with whole grains, and a quarter with protein. It's a rough guide, not a rule, but it makes balanced meals easy to eyeball.

  • Half: vegetables and fruit. Aim for a variety of colors.
  • A quarter: whole grains. Brown rice, oats, whole wheat bread.
  • A quarter: protein. Beans, fish, eggs, poultry, tofu.

Eat more plants

Vegetables, fruit, beans, nuts and whole grains provide fiber, vitamins and minerals, and most people don't get enough of them. You don't have to go fully vegetarian. Adding one extra serving of vegetables to lunch and dinner is a solid start.

Choose whole grains when you can

Whole grains keep the fiber-rich outer layers of the grain, which helps you feel full longer and supports steady energy. Easy swaps include brown rice for white rice, whole wheat pasta, and oatmeal instead of sugary cereal.

Watch the sugary drinks

Soda, sweetened coffee drinks and energy drinks can add a surprising amount of sugar without making you feel full. Water is the best default. If plain water bores you, try adding lemon, cucumber or mint.

Five easy habits to try this week

  • Add one vegetable to every dinner.
  • Keep fruit where you can see it.
  • Swap one sugary drink for water each day.
  • Read the ingredient list, not just the front of the package.
  • Plan two or three meals ahead so you're not deciding when hungry.

Be kind to yourself

Healthy eating isn't about being perfect. One pizza night won't undo your progress, just like one salad won't fix everything. What matters is what you do most of the time. Start small, keep what works, and build from there.
